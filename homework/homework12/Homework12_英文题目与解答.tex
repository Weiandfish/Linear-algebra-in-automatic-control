\section{Problem 1}
Prove Theorem 5.10. For $A\in\C^{m\times n}$ of rank $r$, show that $A=BC$ with $B\in\C^{m\times r}$ of full column rank and $C\in\C^{r\times n}$ of full row rank. Give a practical procedure for obtaining invertible $P,Q$ and verify the ranks in\par
\[
 PAQ=\begin{pmatrix}I_r&D\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B=P^{-1}\binom{I_r}{0},\qquad C=[\,I_r\ D\,]Q^{-1}.
\]
\par\Needspace{4\baselineskip}\textbf{Solution}\par
Find pivot columns by elimination. Permute them to the front: $AQ=[B_0\ N]$. Since $B_0$ spans the column space, $N=B_0D$.\par
Row-reduce $[B_0\mid I_m]\longrightarrow[\binom{I_r}{0}\mid P]$. Then $PN=\binom D0$, so\par
\[
 A=P^{-1}\binom{I_r}{0}[\,I_r\ D\,]Q^{-1}=BC.
\]
The identity minors give rank $r$, preserved by $P^{-1},Q^{-1}$. For $r=0$, take empty factors.\par
\section{Problem 2}
Find a full rank factorization of the following matrix. Display the transformations $T_1,T_2$ clearing the first and third pivot columns, $P=T_2T_1$, $P^{-1}$, and the two factors.\par
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&1&1&4\\2&4&3&0&8\\3&6&4&1&12\end{pmatrix}.
\]
\par\Needspace{4\baselineskip}\textbf{Solution}\par
Apply $R_2\gets R_2-2R_1,\ R_3\gets R_3-3R_1$, then $R_1\gets R_1-R_2,\ R_3\gets R_3-R_2$:\par
\[
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\-2&1&0\\-3&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
T_1A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&1&1&4\\0&0&1&-2&0\\0&0&1&-2&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
T_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&0\\0&1&0\\0&-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
\[
 P=\begin{pmatrix}3&-1&0\\-2&1&0\\-1&-1&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 P^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\2&3&0\\3&4&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 PA=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&0&3&4\\0&0&1&-2&0\\0&0&0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Take the first two columns of $P^{-1}$ and the nonzero rows of $PA$:\par
\[
 \boxed{A=BC=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\2&3\\3&4\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}1&2&0&3&4\\0&0&1&-2&0\end{pmatrix}} .
\]

\section{Problem 3}
Prove Lemma 5.1: for every complex matrix $A$, $\rank(AA^*)=\rank(A^*A)=\rank A$.\par
\par\Needspace{4\baselineskip}\textbf{Solution}\par
For every $x\in\C^n$,\par
\[
 A^*Ax=0\ \Longrightarrow\ 0=x^*A^*Ax=\|Ax\|^2
 \ \Longrightarrow\ Ax=0,\qquad \ker(A^*A)=\ker A.
\]
Rank-nullity gives $\rank(A^*A)=\rank A$ and similarly $\rank(AA^*)=\rank A^*$. Since $\ker A^*=(\im A)^\perp$, $\rank A^*=\rank A$.\par
